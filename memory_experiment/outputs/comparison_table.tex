\section{What we did}

A pretrained \textbf{12-layer, 512-dim GPT-NeoX} was trained to execute
\emph{decision chains}: vectors $v_0 \in \mathbb{Z}_{10}^6$ generate 3--5
blocks, at each step one of two decision functions $f, g$ picks the next
letter-op. The two functions are:
\[
  f(v) = \begin{cases} 10 + v_1 & v_0\text{ even} \\ v_1 & \text{else} \end{cases}
  \qquad
  g(v) = \begin{cases} 10 + v_4 & v_3\text{ even} \\ v_4 & \text{else} \end{cases}
\]
Pretraining uses a fair coin flip between $f$ and $g$. The resulting model
picks each one $\approx 50\%$ of the time per step.

\textbf{Goal: steer the model to always pick $f$ without modifying the
base model.}

\textbf{Memory module.} One attention head with trainable keys
$K \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$, values $V \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$,
query projection $W_q \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, and optional scalar
gate $\gamma$:
\[
  h' \;=\; h \;+\; \gamma \cdot \mathrm{softmax}\!\bigl(W_q h \cdot K^\top / \sqrt{d}\bigr)\,V
\]
Hooked at layer $L$; base model frozen. At $N{=}32$, $d{=}512$: 294K
trainable parameters ($\approx 1\%$ of the base model).

\textbf{Training.} Full-sequence CE on 3{,}000 mixed traces, with labels
overridden at decision points to $f(v)$'s letter. AdamW, lr $3 \times
10^{-3}$, batch size 4, 10 epochs. $\sim$3 minutes on one A100.

\textbf{Finetuning baselines.} Full FT (37.9M params) and LoRA rank-8
(294K params) in two supervision modes: \textbf{A} (ffff-only data, GT
labels --- standard LM loss) and \textbf{B} (all data with $f$-override at
decision points --- same signal the memory uses).

\section{Evaluation}

Two validation sets, both disjoint from training on both letter tuples
and input vectors:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{val\_ffff}} --- 1{,}998 all-$f$ examples,
    chain lengths 3--5 uniform.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{val\_full}} --- 2{,}000 mixed-pattern examples.
    The \emph{honest} alignment test: the model encounters hidden states
    from coin-flip histories the memory may not have trained on.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Metrics} (all evaluated via greedy generation):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{f-selection (\%)} --- fraction of decision-point
    \emph{steps} where the model picked $f$'s letter. Per-step average,
    so longer chains contribute more. Random baseline $\approx 52.5\%$.

  \item \textbf{Full alignment (\%)} --- fraction of \emph{examples}
    where \emph{every} step picked $f$'s letter. Strict conjunction: one
    wrong step scores zero. Baseline $\approx 8.8\%$ on mixed val (expected
    $0.525^n$ over chain lengths 3--5).

  \item \textbf{Op.\ accuracy (\%)} --- fraction of blocks with correct
    arithmetic (we re-execute each chosen letter's transformation and
    compare block text). Must not regress. Baseline $\approx 94.8\%$.
\end{itemize}

($-10$\,pp op.\ accuracy).

effectiveness when injected too close to the output.

\textbf{Order of importance}: (1) the $f$-override at decision points is
the actual alignment signal --- without it, mixed training does nothing
($A5$, $\Delta \approx 0$); (2) mixing the training distribution matters
--- ffff-only training generalises poorly to off-path hidden states
($A2$); (3) full-sequence CE is essential --- DP-only CE breaks
arithmetic catastrophically ($A3$); (4) the gate contributes a small,
consistent boost ($A4$).

\section{Takeaways}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{A small attention-head-shaped module, attached at an
    early residual layer, matches or beats full finetuning on alignment
    while preserving arithmetic roughly 4$\times$ better.} The gain is
    architectural, not a consequence of matching supervision: LoRA at
    the same parameter count with the same $f$-override signal loses by
    $\sim$4\,pp on op.\ accuracy.

  \item \textbf{$N{=}1$ is sufficient for high alignment and
    \emph{improves} arithmetic.} Combined with the linear probe findings
    earlier in the project, this is consistent with the hypothesis that
    the $f$/$g$ preference is controlled by (approximately) a single
    direction in layer-2 residual space.

  \item \textbf{Full FT with mode B breaks.} With 37.9M free parameters,
    the override-on-mixed-data signal causes catastrophic forgetting
    ($-10$\,pp op.\ accuracy). LoRA on the same supervision stays stable
    because the adapter can only move the model in a low-rank subspace.

  \item \textbf{Early layers dominate.} L$\in\{1,2\}$ are tied optimal.
    L$=10$ (high variance across seeds) barely works. This suggests
    that for inference-time alignment of this kind, the correction
    should be injected where the downstream network has most room to
    propagate it.
\end{enumerate}
